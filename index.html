📖 The Basics — start here

Before patterns, before strategies: what you're actually looking at. Ten minutes here makes everything else in the Dojo readable.
1 · One candle = one round of the fight. Each candle summarizes all the buying and selling in a fixed slice of time (in our replay, 5 minutes). A full US trading day, 9:30 AM to 4:00 PM New York, is 78 five-minute candles.
2 · Every candle is just four numbers. Open — the first price of that slice. Close — the last. High and Low — the furthest prices reached. That's it. Everything else in trading is built on these four numbers repeating.
3 · Green vs red. Green (hollow in old books): the candle closed above where it opened — buyers won that round. Red: closed below the open — sellers won. The fat part is the body (open→close). A big body = a decisive round; a tiny body = a coin toss.
4 · Wicks are rejections. The thin lines above and below the body (wicks or shadows) show where price went but couldn't stay. A long lower wick means sellers pushed price down and buyers slammed it back up — that price was rejected. Wicks are often more informative than bodies: they show where the losing side gave up.
5 · Volume is the lie detector. The bars along the bottom of a chart show how many shares traded per candle. A price move on huge volume = lots of real money agreed. The same move on tiny volume = nobody showed up, and it often reverses. Never read price without glancing at volume.
6 · Support and resistance. Prices where the fight keeps repeating: a support is a floor where buyers keep showing up; a resistance is a ceiling where sellers keep appearing. They matter because thousands of traders remember them — yesterday's close, round numbers, the day's opening range. Most patterns in the flashcards are just support/resistance stories.
7 · VWAP — the day's fair price. The orange line in our replay is the Volume-Weighted Average Price: the average price of every share traded today. Big institutions measure their fills against it, so it acts like a magnet and a battleground. Above VWAP = buyers in control today; below = sellers.
8 · How to read any chart in ten seconds. In order: ① Which way is it trending — higher highs or lower lows? ② Is price above or below VWAP? ③ Is volume confirming or sleeping? ④ Only then look for a pattern. Pattern-first reading is how beginners see shapes that aren't there.

📚 Learn Patterns

One pattern at a time: the shape, the story behind it, the trade rule, and — most importantly — the real situations where you'll actually meet it. When these feel familiar, the flashcards will make them permanent.

🎬 Market Replay

A real past trading day, replayed 5 minutes at a time — genuine Yahoo Finance data, future hidden. You get a $10,000 paper account. Buys are auto-sized to risk $100 (1%) with a stop under the recent low, exactly like the academy taught. The market already wrote the answer key; let's see how you do against it.
Pick a symbol and load a day. The chart starts 15 minutes after the open — mark the opening range in your head before trading it.

📜 The Rules (quick reference)

Condensed from the NEXUS Day-Trade Academy. Read them before every replay session until they're reflexes.
1 · Plan before the open. Entry trigger, stop, target — written down. No plan = no trade, and a no-trade day is a winning day.
2 · The stop is decided before the entry. If you can't say where you're wrong, you don't have a trade — you have a feeling.
3 · Risk 1% per trade, max. Shares = dollar risk ÷ (entry − stop). The replay sim sizes this way automatically so it becomes muscle memory.
4 · Two losses = done for the day. Close the laptop. Revenge trading is how one bad morning becomes a bad month.
5 · Never widen a stop, never average down. A day trade that becomes "hold and hope" is a loss wearing a costume.
6 · First 15 minutes: watch, don't chase. Unless you're executing a planned opening-range breakout, the open is where beginners get chopped up.
7 · Judge the plan-following, not the P&L. A losing trade that followed the plan is a good trade. A winning trade that broke the rules is a future disaster on payroll.
8 · Under $25k = PDT limits are real. Max 3 day trades per rolling 5 business days in a US margin account. Practice here costs nothing and has no limits.
